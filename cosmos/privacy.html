2. The short version

Apex Cosmos has no user account, no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting service that we added or could evaluate. We do not run a server of our own that collects or evaluates information about you. For three kinds of data, the clouds in "My planet" (switched off by default), the orbit data of the space stations ISS and Tiangong, and the space weather data (aurora forecast, Kp index and solar flares), we provide files in rented web space at IONOS in Germany (sections 3.4a, 3.8 and 3.8a). We have removed the transport service that Google's billing library brings along by default (section 3.11a). We do not evaluate any information about you; the connection data of these requests is processed by IONOS on our behalf (section 4). Only if you make a purchase do we receive from Google the order details we need for accounting and tax purposes (section 3.11). What the app retrieves from the internet are images, maps and forecast data from public sources of NASA, ESA and NOAA and, via our web space, orbit data from CelesTrak, space weather data from NOAA and, with clouds switched on, a cloud map prepared by us. With each of these retrievals, as with any access on the internet, the IP address of your device is transmitted to the respective server.

Two things the app reads on your device do say something about you: the motion sensor (kept in memory only, section 3.1) and (only if you switch it on explicitly) the coarse device location (kept on the device only, section 3.9). Neither leaves your device. The rest of this policy explains that in detail.

Providing this data is required neither by law nor by contract. You are under no obligation; if you do not switch a feature on, only that feature is unavailable and the rest of the app keeps working.

3. Processing in detail

3.0 What every internet request transmits

This applies to all requests described in section 3: the server receives the IP address of your device, the time, the requested file and an identifier of the app in the form "ApexCosmos/version number (Android live wallpaper; +https://apexstudios-de.github.io/)". That identifier names the app, its version and our website as a way to contact us, not your device and not you; it is necessary so that the archive operators can recognise and contact the app if there is a problem instead of blocking an anonymous application. From the IP address and the identifier together, an operator can recognise for short periods that requests come from the same connection. From which requests happen at all, an operator can also infer which features you have switched on, for example that you use notifications about passes. The individual cases are described in the sections below.

3.1 Running the wallpaper (motion sensor)

If you use a photo as wallpaper, the app reads your device's motion sensor (rotation vector or accelerometer) to move the image when you tilt the device. Of the live wallpapers, only "Solar Apex" (section 3.4g, while "Tilt" is switched on there) and "Black Hole" (section 3.4h, while "Gyro" is switched on there) use it. These values are processed in memory only; they are neither stored nor transmitted. You can switch the feature off in the settings; the sensor is then not registered at all.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the function you requested).

3.2 Image search

When you search for images in the app, your search term is sent to the image archives of NASA (images-api.nasa.gov, USA) and ESA (esahubble.org, esawebb.org); preview images and image sizes of the results come from images-assets.nasa.gov. The respective server receives your IP address and the time of the request. The app does not store a search history. A search only happens when you start it. The archives are in English. If you enter a German search term, the app translates it into the English technical term before sending; the transmitted request therefore reveals that the search was made in German. Whatever you type into the search field is sent to the archive unchanged. Do not enter anything there that you would not entrust to a third-party server. The preview images of the ESA results are loaded from cdn.esahubble.org and cdn.esawebb.org. These addresses belong to ESA but are served through the CDN77 delivery network; its server therefore also receives your IP address and the time of the request.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.3 Image download

When you download an image, it is loaded from the archive (images-assets.nasa.gov, esahubble.org, esawebb.org) onto your device. The server receives your IP address in the process. The image is stored on your device.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4 Live wallpapers "The Sun, now" and "Earth from space"

If you choose one of these two wallpapers, the app fetches the current image automatically and repeatedly. It fetches the Sun about once a day from the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (services.swpc.noaa.gov, USA): first a short list of the available images (about 2 kilobytes), then - only if a newer one is available since the last request - the image itself (about 0.5 to 1.2 megabytes). The app picks the time of day for this once at random and stores it only on your device, so that not all devices ask at the same time. It fetches the Earth about every 6 hours from epic.gsfc.nasa.gov (NASA). Each time, the IP address of your device and the time are transmitted. The address of the Sun image contains the observation channel you selected. The regular requests are skipped in battery saver mode, when the display has been off for more than an hour, and, if you set it so, outside Wi-Fi. As soon as you choose another wallpaper, they stop. If you open the preview of a live wallpaper or change the observation channel of the Sun, the app fetches the corresponding image immediately, even if that wallpaper is not set; for the Earth, Mars and the cloud map at most once every half hour, for the Sun only if no image of that channel was loaded in the last 24 hours. The wallpaper "The Moon, tonight" is computed entirely on the device and needs no internet connection.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the function you chose consists precisely of keeping the image current).

3.4a Live wallpaper "My planet"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe centred on a country you pick from a list. No country is preselected; until you pick one, the globe shows the country of your language setting. Only the country code is stored on the device. If you choose "My location" instead of a country, the globe shows the country your device is currently in; for this the app reads the coarse device location as described in section 3.9. When you choose this wallpaper, the app fetches in the background: the Earth textures for the current month from eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov (NASA, USA; once a month, about 10 MB the first time; plus the sharper Earth map at about 28 MB per month, over Wi-Fi or, only if you enable "Sharper Earth map over mobile data too", over mobile data too), with the space station enabled its orbit data at most every 20 hours (a few kilobytes) from our web space at IONOS (clouds.apexcosmos.de, section 4; the data come from CelesTrak, the app does not contact CelesTrak itself), and with the aurora enabled the aurora forecast (OVATION model, a grid of 360 x 181 probabilities) every hour from the same web space at IONOS (about 3 kilobytes; if the forecast is unavailable, the Kp index of about 1 kilobyte instead). The data come from the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center; we fetch them there centrally once an hour, the app does not contact NOAA for this itself. Each request transmits the IP address of your device and the time. Space station and aurora can be switched off; those requests then stop. Sun, Moon, the day and night line and the times are computed on the device. Clouds: If you switch on the clouds in "My planet" (off by default), the app checks a small companion file with the capture time about once an hour (under 1 kilobyte). It downloads the cloud map, a greyscale image of about 400 kilobytes, only when a newer capture is available, i.e. about every three hours. Both files are hosted at clouds.apexcosmos.de in our web space at IONOS (section 4), which delivers them on our behalf. We create the cloud map automatically from public NOAA satellite imagery (Meteosat, GOES, Himawari); the app itself does not contact NOAA for this. With each request IONOS receives the details listed in section 3.0 (IP address, time, requested file, app identifier), but no location information from the app and no further information about you. The requests only take place while "My planet" is set as your wallpaper or while you open its preview, and only over Wi-Fi unless you have allowed requests over mobile data. If you switch the clouds off, they stop.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4b Live wallpaper "Mars"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe of Mars, centred on a landing site you choose from a list. The map, the terrain, Mars time, the day and night line and the orbits of the moons are part of the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper works without the internet. If you choose this wallpaper, the app fetches in the background, about once a day at a randomly chosen time that is stored only on the device, the current position of the rovers Perseverance and Curiosity from mars.nasa.gov (NASA, USA; under 2 kilobytes each) and at most once a week their driving track (about 600 kilobytes each). The sharp map tiles are part of the app; they are not fetched from the internet. Each request transmits the IP address of your device and the time; the request contains no location and no information about you.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4d Live wallpaper "Venus"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe of Venus. Relief, place names and clouds are contained in the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper downloads nothing from the internet and creates no files. The only things stored are which place on Venus you chose and your display choices (such as zoom, view, clouds on or off). "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4e Live wallpaper "Io"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe of Jupiter's moon Io in front of Jupiter. Map, relief, place names and the image of Jupiter are entirely contained in the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper downloads nothing from the internet and creates no files. Only the place on Io you have chosen and your display choices (such as zoom, view, Jupiter, volcanoes) are stored. "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.

3.4f Live wallpaper "Proxima b"

Relief, starry sky and zone names are entirely contained in the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper downloads nothing from the internet and creates no files. Only the scenario and zone you have chosen and your display choices (such as zoom, view, clouds, flares) are stored. "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.

3.4g Live wallpaper "Solar Apex"

Only your display choices (such as time mode, view, distances, trail length) and the moment from which the time lapse runs are stored. If "Tilt" is switched on, the app reads the motion sensor as described in section 3.1, in memory only. "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.

3.5 Storage on the device

The app's protected storage area holds:

  • your settings, including the images marked as favourites and the time of the last automatic image change,
  • downloaded images together with title, credit and source address,
  • the images of the Sun and Earth live wallpapers,
  • the Earth textures for "My planet" (up to about 38 MB per month),
  • with clouds switched on, the most recently downloaded cloud map and its capture time (about 400 KB),
  • for "Mars", the rover positions,
  • the orbit, Kp and aurora data of whichever features are switched on,
  • the state of the downloads (time of the last attempt, error count and the randomly chosen download time); it is not part of the backup,
  • with push notifications enabled, the planned events of the coming days together with the times and compass directions computed for your place,
  • the note that the app has already asked for a rating (section 3.10a); it is not part of the backup,
  • and, only with the device location enabled, that location in a separate file (section 3.9).

None of this is transmitted to us. Within the app you can delete downloaded images individually. The images, Earth textures and cloud map of a wallpaper that you have set neither on the home screen nor on the lock screen are cleared by the app itself after 90 days without use; if you choose that wallpaper again later, it fetches what it needs again. Under "Settings > App > Storage" you can see how much space each of these takes up and delete it right away; there, too, only for wallpapers you are not currently using, so that a running wallpaper is never left without its data. Everything else stays on the device until you remove it through the Android settings for the app ("Storage" > "Clear data") or by uninstalling. Images, textures, the cloud map and the orbit, Kp and aurora data contain no information about you; the planned events and the device location relate to your place and are deleted as described in sections 3.8 and 3.9. If you enable the automatic image change, the app switches the image after the interval you chose, or when the screen is switched off. To do so it observes whether the screen is turned on or off; the only thing stored is the time of the last change. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is transmitted. Access to this storage is necessary to provide the functions you requested (Section 25(2) no. 2 of the German TDDDG).

3.6 Backup by Android (Auto Backup)

If you have enabled data backup in Android, the system backs up the app's settings file with Google and transfers it when you move to a new device. It contains, among other things, the country chosen for "My planet" or the choice "My location" (without the location itself), the country chosen in the "Sky" tab for sky events (but not the device location, section 3.9), the chosen Mars landing site, the chosen place on Venus, on Io and on Proxima b, which kinds of sky events you want to be notified about and, if you changed them, the times of your quiet hours, that is, when you do not want to be disturbed. It also contains the images you marked as favourites in the gallery, the settings of the automatic image change, and some technical timestamps and counters of the app, such as when the wallpaper was last visible, when an image was last changed automatically, and which notifications have already been sent today. Together these give a rough picture of which images you prefer and when you used the app. Not backed up are: downloaded images, live images, textures, the planned events and the device location. Google is responsible for this backup; it sits in your Google account and remains there even after you uninstall the app, until Android discards it after a long period without use or you delete it in your Google account. You control the backup in the Android settings under "Backup".

3.7 Your own photo as wallpaper

If you choose your own photo as wallpaper through the app, it is handed directly to the Android system. The app neither stores nor transmits it.

3.8 Push notifications about sky events

If you switch push notifications on, the app computes on your device which sky events are visible from your place and reminds you of them. The computation runs entirely on the device; we receive nothing. For some event types the app fetches public forecast data: the Kp forecast and the list of recent solar flares (together about 1 kilobyte, at most every three hours) and the orbit data of ISS and Tiangong (at most every 20 hours), both from our web space at IONOS (clouds.apexcosmos.de; after a failure with a growing pause of up to one day). Kp forecast and solar flares come from the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, the orbit data from CelesTrak; we fetch them there centrally, once an hour and about every four hours respectively. Your device contacts neither NOAA nor CelesTrak for this. As with any request on the internet, the IP address of your device and the time are transmitted to the respective server; the request contains no information about you and no location. The fetched files are kept as a copy on your device. The Kp forecast and solar flares are no longer used once our web space has not renewed them for 24 hours, the orbit data until the next successful retrieval, but for seven days at the longest: after that the app discards and deletes it, because the pass times computed from it would no longer be reliable. You then get no notification rather than one with the wrong time. Unlike the live wallpaper images, these requests are not restricted to Wi-Fi; they also happen over mobile data, because only a few kilobytes are involved. The planned events of the next seven days stay on the device until the next computation. They contain the times and compass directions computed for your place and are deleted as soon as you switch notifications off.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (your consent by switching notifications on); you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future by switching notifications off.

3.8a "Sky" tab (calendar)

The "Sky" tab shows the sky events of the next 30 days for the place you choose at the top of the tab: a country (its centre) or your device location (section 3.9). The same choice applies to push notifications. The list is computed on the device and kept in memory only. When you open the tab, the app fetches (regardless of whether push notifications are enabled, and at most every three hours) the Kp forecast and the list of recent solar flares from our web space at IONOS (clouds.apexcosmos.de, section 4; together about 1 kilobyte; the data come from NOAA, the app does not contact NOAA for this itself). Your device's IP address and the time are transmitted, but no information about you and no location. The files remain on the device as a copy.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.9 Device location (coarse, optional)

If you choose "Use my location" at the top of the "Sky" tab, or "My location" in the country list of "My planet", the app reads the coarse location of your device (about 2 kilometres), when you open the app, at most every 15 minutes, and only while it is open. It is never requested in the background; the wallpaper and notifications only use the value last stored when the app was opened. It is stored on the device (latitude, longitude, time, time zone and the country it lies in) and used only to compute the sky calendar and, if enabled, push notifications (passes, eclipse and twilight times) for your place, and to show the country you are in on "My planet". It is not transmitted to anyone and is excluded from the Android backup and from the transfer to a new device. The stored location is deleted

  • as soon as nothing uses it any more, i.e. a country is picked both in the "Sky" tab and for "My planet": immediately or the next time you open the app,
  • if you have not refreshed it for 30 days: the next time you open the app,
  • if you revoke location access in the Android settings: the next time you open the app,
  • and when you uninstall the app.

If you switch push notifications off, the events computed from it are deleted immediately; the location stays as long as the calendar or "My planet" uses it.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; withdraw at any time by picking a country in both places or by revoking the location permission in the Android settings.

3.10 Handing over to other apps on your device

If you tap "Add to calendar", the app passes the event's title, description (depending on the event including the compass directions and altitudes computed for your place, for example for passes, planets and sunrise and sunset), time and time zone to your calendar app. The entry is only created once you save it there; whether your calendar app syncs it with an online account depends on that app's settings and privacy terms. If you tap "Source", the archive's page opens in your browser; the privacy policy of that archive applies. The buttons for sharing, rating and writing on the "App" page of the settings hand a link to the Play Store to the app you choose, or open the Play Store or your email app. The app itself sends nothing in doing so. The "Rate the app" button only opens the Play Store page; the one-time request for a rating is described in section 3.10a.

3.10a One-time request for a rating

No earlier than three days after installation, and right after you have successfully set a wallpaper, the app asks you at most once, through the Google Play Store, for a rating. To do so it reads from Android the date the app was installed and asks the "Google Play Store" app on your device to show the rating window for Apex Cosmos. The window comes from Google; Google decides whether it appears. The app asks no question of its own beforehand, and no feature of the app, Pro included, depends on whether or how you rate.

The app itself sends nothing to the internet in doing so. The "Google Play Store" app processes the request with your Google account. If you submit a rating, it sends the stars and text to Google; Google publishes the review on the Play Store under the name of your Google profile. As long as the app is only offered in a closed test, Google does not publish the review but only forwards it to us. Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland, is responsible for this under its own privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). You can change or delete your review there at any time.

The app learns neither whether the window was shown nor whether or how you rated. On the device it stores only that it has asked (a yes/no, no time), in a file excluded from Android backup; that is why it does not ask a second time. The note disappears when you clear the app data or uninstall; after a reinstall the app may therefore ask once more.

Like every review on the Play Store, published reviews are visible to us in our Google Play console, where we can reply publicly.


Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is receiving feedback on the app and making the app discoverable on the Play Store. Storing the note and reading the installation date serve solely to avoid asking you too early or more than once (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

5. Transfers to third countries

NASA's servers are located in the USA. The transfer takes place because you retrieve the image from there; only the technically necessary connection data (IP address, time, requested file) are transmitted, no further data about you. NASA is a US federal agency; an adequacy decision of the EU Commission does not apply to it. The same applies to the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center; its servers are located in the USA as well. It is contacted only when the wallpaper "The Sun, now" is chosen; aurora, Kp and solar flare data come from our web space at IONOS in the EU. Where the servers of the ESA archives and of the delivery network for the preview images are located is not known to us. A transfer to a third country can therefore not be ruled out there. IONOS delivers the cloud map, the orbit data and the space weather data from data centres in the EU; no transfer to a third country takes place for them. The bundled images and the wallpapers "The Moon, tonight", "Venus", "Io", "Proxima b", "Solar Apex" and "Black Hole" work without requests to NASA and NOAA. At start-up the app asks the Google Play Store on your device whether you own Pro (section 3.11); Google processes data for this under its own privacy policy.

Google may also process purchase, rating and backup data in the USA. According to Google the transfer relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and additionally on standard contractual clauses; we have not verified this ourselves.

6. Retention

On the device, unless stated otherwise above: until you delete the data or the app. In detail:

  • Settings: until you clear the app data or uninstall.
  • Downloaded images: until you delete them in the app.
  • Live wallpaper images and textures: until replaced by a newer image; if they belong to a wallpaper you are not using, at most 90 days.
  • Cloud map: until replaced by a newer one; if it belongs to a wallpaper you are not using, at most 90 days.
  • Connection data of the cloud, orbit data and space weather requests at IONOS: at most seven days (log files of the web space, according to the IONOS data processing agreement).
  • Planned events: until the next computation, at most seven days, and immediately when you switch push notifications off.
  • Orbit, Kp and aurora data: until the next retrieval. Orbit data for the ISS and Tiangong is additionally deleted once it is more than seven days old; beyond that, the computed pass times would no longer be reliable.
  • Device location: at most 30 days, otherwise as described in section 3.9.
  • Note of the rating request: until you clear the app data or uninstall. Published reviews: at Google, until you delete them (section 3.10a).
  • Order details of a purchase: for as long as statutory tax retention obligations require (currently eight years for accounting records, Section 147(3) AO); they are deleted afterwards. The purchase status on the device: until you clear the app data or uninstall.
  • Backup in your Google account: as described in section 3.6, under Google's responsibility.
  • E-mail enquiries: as described in section 7a.

On the archives' servers: according to their rules, over which we have no influence.

9. App permissions

  • Internet and network state: for search, download and live wallpapers.
  • Set wallpaper: to put an image on the lock screen.
  • Billing through Google Play (com.android.vending.BILLING): comes with the billing library and is used only to purchase Apex Cosmos Pro (section 3.11).
  • Notifications (Android 13 and later, requested only when you switch push notifications on): for hints about sky events such as full moon, space station passes, meteor showers, eclipses or aurora. Events are computed on the device, for the place you chose in the "Sky" tab: the centre of a country or your device location (next item). Nothing about you is sent to any server. Off by default.
  • Location (coarse, about 2 km; only on request): if you choose "Use my location" in the "Sky" tab or "My location" for "My planet", the app may read the coarse location. It is read only while the app is open (at most every 15 minutes), stored on the device and used for sky events (calendar, passes, eclipses, aurora) and the country shown on the globe. No background location, no transmission, no sharing. Picking a country in both places instead deletes the stored location. Off by default.

The Play Store permission list additionally shows "prevent device from sleeping", "run at startup" and "run foreground service". The app does not request these itself; they come from the Android component that schedules the recurring retrievals and the event planning (AndroidX WorkManager) and are used for nothing else. The motion sensor requires no permission. Apart from the coarse location described above, the app requests no access to contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files; you choose your own photo via the Android photo picker, which shows the app only that one file. The app uses no advertising ID and no device identifier.
