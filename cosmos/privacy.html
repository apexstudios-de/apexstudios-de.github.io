2. The short version

Apex Cosmos has no user account, no server of its own, no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting service that we added or could evaluate. Google's billing library does, however, bring along components that can send data to Google (section 3.11a). The app sends no data to us. Only if you make a purchase do we receive from Google the order details we need for accounting and tax purposes (section 3.11). What it retrieves from the internet are images, maps and forecast data from public sources of NASA, ESA, NOAA and CelesTrak – and with each retrieval, as with any access on the internet, the IP address of your device is transmitted to the respective server.

Two things the app reads on your device do say something about you: the motion sensor (kept in memory only, section 3.1) and – only if you switch it on explicitly – the coarse device location (kept on the device only, section 3.9). Neither leaves your device. The rest of this policy explains that in detail.

Providing this data is required neither by law nor by contract. You are under no obligation; if you do not switch a feature on, only that feature is unavailable and the rest of the app keeps working.

3. Processing in detail

3.0 What every internet request transmits

This applies to all requests described in section 3: the server receives the IP address of your device, the time, the requested file and an identifier of the app in the form "ApexCosmos/version number (Android live wallpaper; +https://apexstudios-de.github.io/)". That identifier names the app, its version and our website as a way to contact us, not your device and not you; it is necessary so that the archive operators can recognise and contact the app if there is a problem instead of blocking an anonymous application. From the IP address and the identifier together, an operator can recognise for short periods that requests come from the same connection. From which requests happen at all, an operator can also infer which features you have switched on – for example that you use notifications about passes. The individual cases are described in the sections below.

3.1 Running the wallpaper (motion sensor)

If you use a photo as wallpaper, the app reads your device's motion sensor (rotation vector or accelerometer) to move the image when you tilt the device. The live wallpapers do not use it. These values are processed in memory only; they are neither stored nor transmitted. You can switch the feature off in the settings; the sensor is then not registered at all.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the function you requested).

3.2 Image search

When you search for images in the app, your search term is sent to the image archives of NASA (images-api.nasa.gov, USA) and ESA (esahubble.org, esawebb.org). The respective server receives your IP address and the time of the request. The app does not store a search history. A search only happens when you start it. The archives are in English. If you enter a German search term, the app translates it into the English technical term before sending; the transmitted request therefore reveals that the search was made in German. Whatever you type into the search field is sent to the archive unchanged – do not enter anything there that you would not entrust to a third-party server. The preview images of the ESA results are loaded from cdn.esahubble.org and cdn.esawebb.org. These addresses belong to ESA but are served through the CDN77 delivery network; its server therefore also receives your IP address and the time of the request.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.3 Image download

When you download an image, it is loaded from the archive (images-assets.nasa.gov, esahubble.org, esawebb.org) onto your device. The server receives your IP address in the process. The image is stored on your device.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4 Live wallpapers "The Sun, now" and "Earth from space"

If you choose one of these two wallpapers, the app fetches the current image automatically and repeatedly from NASA servers: the Sun from sdo.gsfc.nasa.gov about every 30 minutes, the Earth from epic.gsfc.nasa.gov about every 6 hours. Each time, the IP address of your device and the time are transmitted. The address of the Sun image contains the observation channel you selected and, so that the resolution matches your screen, whether your screen is at least 1000 pixels wide. The regular requests are skipped in battery saver mode, when the display has been off for more than an hour, and – if you set it so – outside Wi-Fi. As soon as you choose another wallpaper, they stop. If you open the preview of a live wallpaper or change the observation channel of the Sun, the app fetches the corresponding image once, immediately, even if that wallpaper is not set. The wallpaper "The Moon, tonight" is computed entirely on the device and needs no internet connection.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the function you chose consists precisely of keeping the image current).

3.4a Live wallpaper "My planet"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe centred on a country you pick from a list. No country is preselected. Only the country code is stored on the device. If you choose "My location" instead of a country, the globe shows the country your device is currently in; for this the app reads the coarse device location as described in section 3.9. When you choose this wallpaper, the app fetches in the background: the Earth textures for the current month from eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov (NASA, USA; once a month, about 10 MB the first time; with the "High-resolution texture" option enabled an additional 28 MB or so per month, over Wi-Fi or, only if you enable "Also download over mobile data", over mobile data too), with the space station enabled its orbit data at most every 20 hours from celestrak.org (USA, a few kilobytes), and with the aurora enabled the aurora forecast (OVATION model, a grid of 360 x 181 probabilities) every hour from services.swpc.noaa.gov (NOAA, USA, about 150 kilobytes compressed; if the forecast is unavailable, the Kp index of about 5 kilobytes instead). Each request transmits the IP address of your device and the time. Space station and aurora can be switched off; those requests then stop. Sun, Moon, the day and night line and the times are computed on the device.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4b Live wallpaper "Mars"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe of Mars, centred on a landing site you choose from a list. The map, the terrain, Mars time, the day and night line and the orbits of the moons are part of the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper works without the internet. If you choose this wallpaper, the app fetches in the background, at most every twelve hours, the current position of the rovers Perseverance and Curiosity from mars.nasa.gov (NASA, USA; under 2 kilobytes each) and at most once a week their driving track (about 600 kilobytes each). Only if you switch on "Load sharper Mars map" does the app additionally fetch map tiles around the chosen landing site from trek.nasa.gov (NASA Solar System Treks, USA; about 7 megabytes per zoom level, up to about 20 megabytes per site, over Wi-Fi or, only if you switch it on, also over mobile data). They stay on the device; only when the store of 80 megabytes is full are the oldest removed, and a site visited again later is fetched again. If the server repeatedly refuses requests, the app fetches no more tiles in this version. Each request transmits the IP address of your device and the time; the request contains no location and no information about you. It does contain the tile coordinate of the chosen landing site, so the operator can tell which region of Mars you are looking at.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4c Live wallpaper "Plate Tectonics"

This wallpaper shows a computed Earth whose continents drift over 500 million years. The plate model, elevation models and future scenarios are entirely contained in the app; the wallpaper downloads nothing from the internet. If Earth textures from "My planet" are already on the device, it uses them for the colours around the present, but it does not download any itself. You choose the country the camera follows from the same list as for "My planet"; only the country code is stored. "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.4d Live wallpaper "Venus"

This wallpaper shows a computed globe of Venus. Relief, place names and clouds are contained in the app or computed on the device; the wallpaper downloads nothing from the internet and creates no files. The only things stored are which place on Venus you chose and your display choices (such as zoom, view, clouds on or off). "My location" is not offered for this wallpaper.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.5 Storage on the device

The app's protected storage area holds:

  • your settings, including the images marked as favourites and the time of the last automatic image change,
  • downloaded images together with title, credit and source address,
  • the images of the Sun and Earth live wallpapers,
  • the Earth textures for "My planet" (up to about 38 MB per month),
  • for "Mars", the map tiles (up to 80 MB) and the rover positions,
  • the orbit, Kp and aurora data of whichever features are switched on,
  • with push notifications enabled, the planned events of the coming days together with the times and compass directions computed for your place,
  • and, only with the device location enabled, that location in a separate file (section 3.9).

None of this is transmitted to us. Within the app you can delete downloaded images individually. The images, Earth textures and Mars tiles of a wallpaper that you have set neither on the home screen nor on the lock screen are cleared by the app itself after 90 days without use; if you choose that wallpaper again later, it fetches what it needs again. Under "Settings – App – Storage" you can see how much space each of these takes up and delete it right away – there, too, only for wallpapers you are not currently using, so that a running wallpaper is never left without its data. Everything else stays on the device until you remove it through the Android settings for the app ("Storage" – "Clear data") or by uninstalling. These files contain no information about you. If you enable the automatic image change, the app switches the image after the interval you chose, or when the screen is switched off. To do so it observes whether the screen is turned on or off; the only thing stored is the time of the last change. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is transmitted. Access to this storage is necessary to provide the functions you requested (Section 25(2) no. 2 of the German TDDDG).

3.6 Backup by Android (Auto Backup)

If you have enabled data backup in Android, the system backs up the app's settings file with Google and transfers it when you move to a new device. It contains, among other things, the countries chosen for "My planet" and "Plate Tectonics" or the choice "My location" (without the location itself), the country chosen in the "Sky" tab for sky events (but not the device location, section 3.9), the chosen Mars landing site, the chosen place on Venus, which kinds of sky events you want to be notified about and, if you changed them, the times of your quiet hours, that is, when you do not want to be disturbed. It also contains the images you marked as favourites in the gallery, the settings of the automatic image change, and some technical timestamps and counters of the app – such as when the wallpaper was last visible, when an image was last changed automatically, and which notifications have already been sent today. Together these give a rough picture of which images you prefer and when you used the app. Not backed up are: downloaded images, live images, textures, Mars tiles, the planned events and the device location. Google is responsible for this backup; it sits in your Google account and remains there even after you uninstall the app, until Android discards it after a long period without use or you delete it in your Google account. You control the backup in the Android settings under "Backup".

3.7 Your own photo as wallpaper

If you choose your own photo as wallpaper through the app, it is handed directly to the Android system. The app neither stores nor transmits it.

3.8 Push notifications about sky events

If you switch push notifications on, the app computes on your device which sky events are visible from your place and reminds you of them. The computation runs entirely on the device; we receive nothing. For some event types the app fetches public forecast data: the Kp forecast and the list of recent solar flares from the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (services.swpc.noaa.gov, USA; at most every three hours, about 5 kilobytes each) and the orbit data of ISS and Tiangong from CelesTrak (celestrak.org, USA; at most every 20 hours). As with any request on the internet, the IP address of your device and the time are transmitted to the respective server; the request contains no information about you and no location. The fetched files are kept as a copy on your device. The Kp forecast and solar flares are no longer used after 24 hours at most, the orbit data until the next successful retrieval, but for seven days at the longest: after that the app discards and deletes it, because the pass times computed from it would no longer be reliable. You then get no notification rather than one with the wrong time. Unlike the live wallpaper images, these requests are not restricted to Wi-Fi; they also happen over mobile data, because only a few kilobytes are involved. The planned events of the next seven days stay on the device until the next computation. They contain the times and compass directions computed for your place and are deleted as soon as you switch notifications off.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (your consent by switching notifications on); you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future by switching notifications off.

3.8a "Sky" tab (calendar)

The "Sky" tab shows the sky events of the next 30 days for the place you choose at the top of the tab: a country (its centre) or your device location (section 3.9). The same choice applies to push notifications. The list is computed on the device and kept in memory only. When you open the tab, the app fetches – regardless of whether push notifications are enabled, and at most every three hours – the Kp forecast and the list of recent solar flares from services.swpc.noaa.gov (NOAA, USA; a few kilobytes each). Your device's IP address and the time are transmitted, but no information about you and no location. The files remain on the device as a copy.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.9 Device location (coarse, optional)

If you choose "Use my location" at the top of the "Sky" tab, or "My location" in the country list of "My planet", the app reads the coarse location of your device (about 2 kilometres), afresh each time you open the app and only while it is open. It is never requested in the background; the wallpaper and notifications only use the value last stored when the app was opened. It is stored on the device (latitude, longitude, time, time zone and the country it lies in) and used only to compute the sky calendar and – if enabled – push notifications (passes, eclipse and twilight times) for your place, and to show the country you are in on "My planet". It is not transmitted to anyone and is excluded from the Android backup and from the transfer to a new device. The stored location is deleted

  • as soon as nothing uses it any more, i.e. a country is picked both in the "Sky" tab and for "My planet": immediately or the next time you open the app,
  • if you have not refreshed it for 30 days: the next time you open the app,
  • if you revoke location access in the Android settings: the next time you open the app,
  • and when you uninstall the app.

If you switch push notifications off, the events computed from it are deleted immediately; the location stays as long as the calendar or "My planet" uses it.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; withdraw at any time by picking a country in both places or by revoking the location permission in the Android settings.

3.10 Handing over to other apps on your device

If you tap "Add to calendar", the app passes the event's title, description – depending on the event including the compass directions and altitudes computed for your place, for example for passes, planets and sunrise and sunset –, time and time zone to your calendar app. The entry is only created once you save it there; whether your calendar app syncs it with an online account depends on that app's settings and privacy terms. If you tap "Source", the archive's page opens in your browser; the privacy policy of that archive applies.

3.11 In-app purchase

The app offers an optional one-time purchase via Google Play (“Apex Cosmos Pro”). Purchase, payment and invoicing are handled by Google through your Google account under Google’s terms and privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy); we do not receive your payment details. On your device, Google Play tells the app whether and what you bought, including the order number and proof of purchase. From this the app stores only whether it is unlocked, whether a payment is still being confirmed and when Google last answered – in a separate file excluded from Android backup; it sends these details to no one. At startup and via “Restore purchase” the app asks Google Play again.

For accounting, refunds and enquiries we receive order details from Google: order number, product, price, taxes, time, status and your country, in some countries also region or postal code. In our Google Play console we can also find an order by the e-mail address of your Google account. These data do not come from you directly but from Google.

Providing them is required neither by law nor by contract; without a purchase the rest of the app remains usable. The app itself passes on nothing about you when you buy: no identifier for your device, none for your account, and not where in the app you started the purchase.


Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (carrying out the purchase and unlocking); for retaining accounting records Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with statutory tax retention obligations, in particular Section 147 of the German Fiscal Code (AO).

4. Recipients

  • NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration, USA): image library, SDO and EPIC images, for the "Mars" wallpaper the rover positions (mars.nasa.gov) and, if you choose so, map tiles (trek.nasa.gov). NASA is itself responsible for the processing on its servers; its privacy notice is available at https://www.nasa.gov/privacy/
  • ESA/Hubble and ESA/Webb (esahubble.org, esawebb.org): image archives. The preview images are delivered by CDN77 (DataCamp Limited, United Kingdom) via cdn.esahubble.org and cdn.esawebb.org. Where the servers of ESA and of the delivery network are located in detail is not known to us.
  • CelesTrak (celestrak.org, USA): orbit data of the space stations ISS and Tiangong, only with "My planet" and the space station enabled or with push notifications for passes enabled.
  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (services.swpc.noaa.gov, USA): aurora forecast (OVATION) and, as a fallback, Kp index, only with "My planet" and the aurora enabled; with push notifications enabled also the three-day Kp forecast (aurora alert) and the list of recent solar flares (flare info), about 5 kilobytes each, at most every three hours; these two also when you open the "Sky" tab.
  • Google: distribution and purchase processing via Google Play (section 3.11), Android data backup if you enabled it, and as the operator of our e-mail inbox if you write to us (section 7a).

Apart from an e-mail you send us yourself and the order details of a purchase, we receive no data.

5. Transfers to third countries

NASA's servers are located in the USA. The transfer takes place because you retrieve the image from there; only the technically necessary connection data (IP address, time, requested file) are transmitted, no further data about you. NASA is a US federal agency; an adequacy decision of the EU Commission does not apply to it. The same applies to CelesTrak and the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center; their servers are located in the USA as well. They are contacted when the space station or the aurora is enabled for "My planet", or when you have enabled push notifications for passes, aurora or solar flares, and – NOAA only – when you open the "Sky" tab. Where the servers of the ESA archives and of the delivery network for the preview images are located is not known to us. A transfer to a third country can therefore not be ruled out there. If you do not want this, use only the bundled images and the Moon wallpaper – these work without an internet connection.

Google may also process purchase, backup and e-mail data in the USA. According to Google the transfer relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and additionally on standard contractual clauses; we have not verified this ourselves.

6. Retention

On the device, unless stated otherwise above: until you delete the data or the app. In detail:

  • Settings: until you clear the app data or uninstall.
  • Downloaded images: until you delete them in the app.
  • Live wallpaper images and textures: until replaced by a newer image; if they belong to a wallpaper you are not using, at most 90 days.
  • Mars tiles: at most 90 days once you no longer use the Mars wallpaper; while you do use it, up to the limit of 80 MB, beyond which the oldest tiles go.
  • Planned events: until the next computation, at most seven days, and immediately when you switch push notifications off.
  • Orbit, Kp and aurora data: until the next retrieval. Orbit data for the ISS and Tiangong is additionally deleted once it is more than seven days old – beyond that, the computed pass times would no longer be reliable.
  • Device location: at most 30 days, otherwise as described in section 3.9.
  • Order details of a purchase: for as long as statutory tax retention obligations require (currently eight years for accounting records, Section 147(3) AO); they are deleted afterwards. The purchase status on the device: until you clear the app data or uninstall.
  • Backup in your Google account: as described in section 3.6, under Google's responsibility.
  • E-mail enquiries: as described in section 7a.

On the archives' servers: according to their rules, over which we have no influence.

9. App permissions

  • Internet and network state: for search, download and live wallpapers.
  • Set wallpaper: to put an image on the lock screen.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and later, requested only when you switch push notifications on): for hints about sky events such as full moon, space station passes, meteor showers, eclipses or aurora. Events are computed on the device, for the place you chose in the "Sky" tab: the centre of a country or your device location (next item). Nothing about you is sent to any server. Off by default.
  • Location (coarse, about 2 km; only on request): if you choose "Use my location" in the "Sky" tab or "My location" for "My planet", the app may read the coarse location. It is read only while the app is open, stored on the device and used for sky events (calendar, passes, eclipses, aurora) and the country shown on the globe. No background location, no transmission, no sharing. Picking a country in both places instead deletes the stored location. Off by default.

The Play Store permission list additionally shows "prevent device from sleeping", "run at startup" and "run foreground service". The app does not request these itself; they come from the Android component that schedules the recurring retrievals and the event planning (AndroidX WorkManager) and are used for nothing else. The motion sensor requires no permission. Apart from the coarse location described above, the app requests no access to contacts, camera, microphone, photos or files; you choose your own photo via the Android photo picker, which shows the app only that one file. The app uses no advertising ID and no device identifier.
