2. The short version

Apex Split has no user account, no server of its own, no advertising and no analytics or crash-reporting service that we added or could evaluate. Your expenses, the names of the participants, your receipts and their photos are stored only on your device. The app sends none of this to us or to anyone else. It leaves your device only when you share something yourself (section 3.6).

Two Google components inside the app do, however, send data to Google: the ML Kit text recognition sends technical usage data, without images and without recognized text (section 3.3), and the Google Play billing library sends its requests to Google Play (section 3.8). Only if you make a purchase do we receive from Google the order details we need for accounting and tax purposes.

Providing data is neither a legal nor a contractual requirement. Whatever you do not enter is missing only from the respective feature; the rest of the app remains usable.

3. Processing in detail

3.1 Expenses, participants and settlement

In the app you create contexts, such as a trip or a shared flat. In them you record participants under the name you enter, and receipts with merchant, date, currency, items, amounts, notes and who paid and who bears which share, as well as exchange rates and settlement payments. For a settlement payment the app stores who paid how much to whom and when, by which method (cash, bank transfer, PayPal or other) and a note if you enter one. This information is kept in a database in the app's protected storage. The app uses it only there, to calculate shares, balances and settlement payments. The names of the participants are information about other people. A first name or nickname is enough; the app needs nothing more. Do not put bank details or anything else into notes that you would not share with the other participants – notes travel along when you send a context (section 3.6). Recurring expenses (after purchase): for rent, power or subscriptions the app stores a rule with description, amount, currency, payer, people sharing, interval and first date. It adds due dates as a regular receipt when you open the app. The rule travels along when you send a context (section 3.6). Saved groups (after purchase): names you save as a regular group, such as "Family", are kept in the same database so the app can insert them when you create a context. Saved groups never leave the device, not even when you send a context.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the feature you requested).

3.2 Receipt photos and PDF receipts

You can photograph a receipt, pick it from your gallery or files, or send it to Apex Split from another app via "Share" (images and PDF). To take a photo, the app opens your device's camera app; Apex Split itself has no camera permission. When picking from your gallery or files, the app sees only the file you choose. If you wish, you can crop the photo before recognition. The app stores a copy of the receipt in its protected storage and links it to the receipt. Temporary copies created by the camera, the picker and cropping are kept in the app's cache; the app deletes them on the first start that takes place at least one hour after they were created.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.3 Text recognition (ML Kit by Google)

Receipt recognition runs entirely on your device. It uses Google's ML Kit Text Recognition; the recognition model is part of the app. Photos, PDF receipts and the recognized text are not transmitted. The app stores the recognized text with the receipt so that you can read it there. According to Google, however, ML Kit sends usage and diagnostic data to Google: information about the device and the app (such as device model, operating system version, package name and app version), a per-installation identifier that according to Google is not intended to uniquely identify users, and performance and error information such as recognition time, image format and error codes. According to Google, it uses this data to measure and improve ML Kit, transmits it encrypted and does not pass it on to third parties. We receive none of it and cannot view it. ML Kit is set up when the app starts; whether data is already sent at that point, even if you do not scan a receipt, is determined by Google. ML Kit offers no setting to switch this transmission off.
Details: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR – ML Kit is the component that performs the on-device receipt recognition you requested and cannot be included without this transmission.

3.4 Settings

In a separate file the app stores your settings: style, accent colour, light or dark mode, language, default currency, how a shared settlement text should look, whether to confirm before deleting and whether to crop before recognition, whether the app lock is on (section 3.9), whether you have unlocked the app, whether a payment is still pending (section 3.8), which contexts you have sent or taken over from others and – only if you enter it – your sender name, which appears in PDF settlements. The components for camera, gallery and cropping also create small settings files of their own in which they note the path of the image they last handled; these stay on the device too.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.5 No Android backup, built-in data backup

Apex Split is excluded from Android backup and from transfer to a new device. Your data therefore does not end up in your Google account. The flip side: if you change devices or uninstall the app, it is lost unless you have made a backup yourself beforehand. For this, Settings → Data backup offers a free full backup (section 3.6). It is only created when you trigger it, and it only goes where you send it in the share menu. When you restore a backup, the app replaces all of its data with the backup's contents. The app remembers when you last made a backup in order to remind you; this, too, stays on the device.

3.6 Sharing

Five features take data out of the app, and only when you trigger them:

  • Settlement as text, for example for WhatsApp: name of the context, names of the participants, amounts, balances and, depending on your settings, open items; at the "By receipt" level also each receipt with date, merchant, items and shares.
  • Settlement as PDF (after purchase): the same information plus your sender name, and depending on the chosen level the individual receipts with merchant, date and items. Your sender name is also stored in the PDF's document properties ("Author" field).
  • Settlement as spreadsheet (CSV, after purchase): two files. The first contains every item with date, receipt, description, quantity, amount, currency, rate, payer, split and each participant's share; the second the balances per person, the outstanding settlement payments and the recorded payments with date and note.
  • Send context: a file containing the whole context – name of the context, participants, receipts with merchant, date, items, amounts and notes, the origin of each receipt (photo, file, entered by hand or recurring), the split, exchange rates, settlement payments with method and note, recurring expenses, and the times at which entries were created and changed. Receipt photos are not included.
  • Data backup (free): a ZIP file with all contexts and the information listed above, plus the raw recognized text of each receipt, your groups of people and part of your settings (appearance, language, default currency, sender name). Receipt photos are included unless you switch them off first. Purchase status and app lock are not included. The file is not encrypted; anyone who can open it can read everything in it. Keep it as safely as the receipts themselves.

The file names contain the name of the context; a PDF for a single person also carries that person's name in the file name and subject. The app hands the text or file to Android's share menu; you choose there where it goes. From then on, the terms of the app you choose and of the recipient apply. We receive nothing. The file created for this is kept in the app's cache; the app deletes it on the first start that takes place at least one hour after it was created. When sharing, you pass on information about the other participants. Share a settlement only with the people it concerns. If, conversely, you receive a context file and take it over, the app stores its content on your device like a context of your own.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.7 Deletion

In the app you can delete individual receipts and whole contexts; the associated receipt photos are deleted with them. You remove all of the app's data via the app's Android settings ("Storage" – "Clear data") or by uninstalling it.

3.8 In-app purchase

The app offers an optional one-time purchase via Google Play. It unlocks unlimited contexts, sending contexts, PDF output and the "Glass" style. Purchase, payment and invoicing are handled by Google via your Google account under Google's terms and privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy); we do not receive your payment details. The app connects to Google Play only when you open the purchase screen – it then loads the price – or tap "Restore purchase", when it asks whether you have already bought it. It does not do so when the app starts. The one exception: if a payment is still pending, for example because your payment method confirms it later, the app connects on the next start to complete the purchase; otherwise Google refunds a purchase after three days. The only things stored are whether the app is unlocked and whether a payment is pending (section 3.4); the app sends this to no one.

For accounting, refunds and enquiries we receive from Google details of each order: order number, product, price, taxes, time, status and your country, in some countries also state or postal code. In our Google Play console we can also find an order by the e-mail address of your Google account. This data does not come from you directly but from Google.

The billing library brings further Google components with it, including a transport service (Android Transport) that can send event data to Google. What is transmitted and when is determined by Google; we receive nothing from it. These components are present even if you buy nothing.


Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (carrying out the purchase and unlocking); for retaining accounting records Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with the statutory retention obligations under German tax law, in particular Section 147 of the German Fiscal Code (AO).

3.9 Data security

Your data is kept in the app's protected storage, which other apps cannot access, and on current devices additionally under Android's encryption. The app has no encryption of its own for the database; please protect your device with a screen lock. The app itself transmits nothing over the internet. According to Google, the transmissions of the Google components (sections 3.3 and 3.8) are encrypted. Your data is excluded from Android backup (section 3.5). In addition, you can turn on an app lock in the settings. The app then asks for your fingerprint, face or device PIN every time you open it, and hides its preview in the recent apps overview (Android 13 and later).

6. Retention

  • Contexts, receipts, receipt photos and settings: on your device until you delete them in the app, clear the app's data or uninstall the app.
  • Temporary copies and files for sharing: until the first start of the app that takes place at least one hour after they were created.
  • Data held by Google from ML Kit and the billing library: under Google's rules, which we cannot influence.
  • Order details of a purchase: as long as statutory tax retention obligations require (currently eight years for accounting records, Section 147(3) AO); they are deleted afterwards.
  • E-mail enquiries: see section 7a.

7. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection (Art. 15–21 GDPR). Since we receive no data from the app, we can usually provide no information about you; you are nevertheless welcome to contact the e-mail address above. All we may hold about you is an e-mail you sent us and the order details of a purchase. For a request, please give us the e-mail address of your Google account or the order number. Order details can only be erased once the retention period has expired; until then we restrict processing to that purpose. You can delete the data on your device yourself (section 3.7) and take it with you per context as a file (section 3.6). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for instance the one responsible for us: Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit, Alt-Moabit 59–61, 10555 Berlin, Germany, https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de

7a. If you write to us

If you write to the address given above, we process your e-mail address and the content of your message in order to answer it. The inbox is operated by Google (Gmail); your message is therefore stored on the servers of a US provider. We delete the message once the enquiry is closed and no retention obligation applies.
Legal basis: for requests concerning your data subject rights Art. 6(1)(c) in conjunction with Art. 12 GDPR, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our interest in being able to answer enquiries about our app).

8. No automated decision-making

There is no profiling and no automated decision-making.

9. App permissions

  • Internet and network state: the app does not request these for its own purposes. They come from the Google components ML Kit and Google Play Billing (sections 3.3 and 3.8). The app itself downloads nothing from the internet and sends nothing there.
  • Google Play Billing: for the optional purchase.
  • Biometrics: only for the optional app lock (section 3.9). Android performs the check. The app only learns whether it passed; fingerprint or face data never reaches the app and is not stored by it.

The app does not request access to the camera, contacts, location, microphone, or all of your photos or files. You take photos with your device's camera app and pick files via the Android picker, which shows the app only the file you choose. The app does not use an advertising ID.

10. Changes
